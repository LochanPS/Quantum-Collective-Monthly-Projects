Quantum Collective · Monthly Project #3

qnoise

A perfect simulator lies to you.
Real quantum computers are noisy.

Take any quantum circuit and see what it actually does on imperfect hardware — decoherence, gate error, and readout error, rendered right in your terminal, side by side with the ideal result.

open source pure Python + NumPy terminal-only 84 tests Apache-2.0
The terminal, before & after

Same data. A UI that earns a screenshot.

The engine was always correct — but the output was flat monochrome text. We rebuilt the render layer: colored panels, sub-character bars, and leakage flagged in red, all degrading cleanly to ASCII on plain consoles.

The money shot

Noise, made visible.

Those two red dots are leakage — outcomes that physically shouldn't exist, conjured by noise. The fidelity gauge shows exactly how far the state drifted; purity falls from a perfect 1.000 as the state decoheres. Then the sampled measurement adds imperfect readout on top.

What's inside

A real physics engine, not a toy.

density matrix
The correct tool for mixed states. ρ → UρU† per gate, ρ → ΣKρK† per channel.
5 channels
Depolarizing, amplitude damping (T1), phase damping (T2), bit-flip, phase-flip.
presets
ibm_ish & ion_ish hardware profiles, plus a custom NoiseModel builder.
readout error
Model imperfect measurement — per qubit — separately from gate noise.
drift metrics
State fidelity, trace distance, total-variation distance, purity gauge.
noise sweep
The decoherence decay curve — fidelity vs rate — built into the CLI.
trust anchor
Noise off reproduces the ideal simulator exactly. 84 tests guard it.
extensible
Add a noise channel in ~15 lines. Beginner → Expert contributor roadmap.
