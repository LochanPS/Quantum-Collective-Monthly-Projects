# Pitch Deck — Additions from the Discussion

Supplementary notes for the 7-slide Quantum Collective Lab Suite deck. Each item
is tagged **[TALK]** (speaker/talking point), **[SLIDE]** (add to the deck), or
**[ROADMAP]** (mention only as planned, not built). All items respect the deck's
existing rails: no commercials on slides, no invented numbers, real hardware
mentioned once (Slide 1), British "practise" throughout.

---

## 1. Prerequisites — [TALK] + light [SLIDE] line

The elective is niche and cannot be picked up cold like front-end or back-end
programming. A prerequisite gate is a feature, not a barrier — it keeps the
cohort ready and the elective credible.

- **Logic + linear algebra** — school/PU-level linear algebra is already taught,
  so this is "should be comfortable with," not a hurdle.
- **Basic Python / any scripting language** — concept-level only, *not* DSA or
  CS-grade coding. Python because that is the language of the real frameworks
  (Qiskit, PennyLane).
- **Not branch-locked** — open to CSE and ECE alike. ECE has an edge, because
  quantum sits at the hardware + software seam. This makes the elective
  defensible for both departments.

**Slide placement:** one muted sub-line on **Slide 6 (Align)** —
"Prerequisite: comfort with logic, school-level linear algebra, and basic
Python — open to CSE and ECE." Also carried in the spoken track.

## 2. Career outcome — [TALK] (honest rail intact)

- Concrete target roles work with **Qiskit** (core quantum operations) and
  **PennyLane** (quantum machine learning) — the two frameworks nearly every
  quantum employer wants right now.
- **The 90/10 framing:** simulator-based learning takes a student ~90% of the
  way; the final 10% is running on real quantum hardware — exactly what research
  and industry do. Reinforces Slide 1's real-hardware point without repeating
  the feature.
- **Honest caveat (keep it — it builds trust):** selection processes (aptitude,
  MCQ, programming rounds) have not changed, and companies are still figuring out
  what they need. So this is an *edge on the student's profile, not a placement
  guarantee.*
- **Real demand signal, no numbers/names:** "Industry demand is concrete — we're
  already fielding hiring interest for quantum work." (Anonymised.)

**Slide placement:** talking-point-grade. If on a slide at all, a muted sub-line
on **Slide 7** — "An early, demonstrable edge in a field employers are actively
hiring into." Never a placement promise.

## 3. Academic integrity — strengthens [SLIDE 3]

Answers the two objections you will hear most: "students will just AI it" and
"faculty are pressured to pass everyone."

- **AI has almost no surface here.** Only one module (Code Lab) takes
  text -> circuit. Everywhere else — circuit design, networks, cryptography — the
  work is manual and hands-on. Even with AI help, a student must execute every
  step inside the software themselves.
- **Role-gated access** — professor login key; students / faculty / admin each
  see a different layer. Reports are stamped (name + register number) and cannot
  be silently forged.
- **The line that answers the pressure problem:** "The tool is wired into the
  exam and can't be done remotely, so 'pass him and move on' isn't available as a
  shortcut — the record reflects what the student actually did." Ties directly
  into the Slide 5 self-proving loop.

## 4. Theory + lab complementarity — [TALK]

"The lab is self-sufficient on its own, but it's designed to complement a theory
course — students learn concepts in class and prove them by doing in the lab."
Helps a college decide between elective-only and a paired theory + lab offering.
