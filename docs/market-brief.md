# Quantum Computing Market & Jobs — Director Meeting Brief

Talking points backed by current (Sept 2026) third-party figures. Market-size
estimates vary widely across research firms, so quote ranges, not single numbers.
Sources listed at the end.

---

## 1. Market size & projections (quote as a range)

- 2026 market: roughly **US $2-5 billion** (varies by firm).
- 2030 forecast: roughly **US $16-20 billion**.
- Growth: **~30-40% CAGR** — firms disagree on the number, agree on the
  trajectory.

Say: "Forecasts put it around $2-5B today, growing to $16-20B by 2030 at ~30-40%
a year. The firms disagree on the figure but not the direction."

---

## 2. Talent gap — the strongest card

- Quantum job postings grew **~270% from 2020 to 2024**.
- **~3 open roles per 1 qualified candidate** worldwide.
- **McKinsey: 40,000+ quantum-expert shortage by 2030.**
- **Over 50% of quantum roles need only a bachelor's degree — no PhD.**
  (The core argument for an undergraduate lab.)

---

## 3. What employers want (maps directly to the lab)

| Employer requirement | Where the lab covers it |
|---|---|
| Python (essential; ~75% of employers rank programming essential) | Python prerequisite + Code Lab |
| Quantum SDKs — Qiskit, Cirq, Q# | Code Lab (Qiskit); PennyLane for QML |
| Linear algebra + probability | Prerequisite |
| Superposition, entanglement, Grover's & Shor's | Circuit Simulator, Algorithm Visualiser, BB84 |

The pitch: the exact skills employers list are the exact things the lab teaches
by doing.

---

## 4. India angle — lead with this for a director

- **National Quantum Mission: Rs 6,003 crore total outlay (2023-2031).**
- **Budget 2026 raised the annual allocation to Rs 900 crore — a 50% increase**
  over the prior year. Government commitment is rising.
- **NQM includes 10,000+ skilling spots** in quantum computing and cryptography;
  thematic hubs across 17 states.
- Colleges are already moving (e.g. a private Kerala engineering college set up a
  dedicated quantum lab via an industry partnership).

Line: "A national mission with growing funding, an explicit skilling mandate, and
a global talent gap where half the jobs don't need a PhD. Colleges that build the
lab now are early — and early is the differentiator."

---

## 5. What to say to the director (the flow)

> "Quantum is on every roadmap and India has a Rs 6,000-crore national mission
> behind it, with the 2026 budget increasing funding 50%. But there's a global
> talent gap — about three open roles for every qualified person, and McKinsey
> projects a 40,000+ shortage by 2030. The important part: more than half those
> roles need only a bachelor's degree. Employers ask for Python, Qiskit and
> linear algebra plus hands-on understanding of the core algorithms — exactly
> what students do in this lab. So a college that runs this now graduates
> students into a market that's short of them, aligned with national policy, as
> an early mover before it becomes standard. To be clear it's an edge on the
> student's profile, not a placement guarantee — companies are still defining
> their hiring — but the direction is not in doubt."

The honest caveat at the end is what makes the rest credible.
